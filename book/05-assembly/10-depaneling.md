# Depaneling

After assembly and testing, the boards are separated from their panel.
V-scored panels are snapped by hand or run through a round-blade cutter; tab-routed panels are cut at each tab with a router, nibbler or hand tool; flexible boards are cut with scissors at low volume or with a laser at higher volume.
Depaneling bends and cuts the board right next to your parts, so it's where brittle parts crack.

Almost everything your design controls here is decided when the panel is designed, so the recommendations live in Part 2:

- Breakaway methods, overhanging parts, tabs and thick boards: [Breakaway methods](../02-the-panel/03-breakaway-methods.md) (PCB-DFM-001, PCB-DFA-010 to 014).
- Distances from break lines, and ceramic capacitors: [Clearance near board and panel edges](../02-the-panel/04-edge-clearance.md) (PCB-DFA-015 to 017).

## PCB-DFA-062: Ask how flexible boards will be separated

**Recommendation:** For flexible boards, agree with your assembler how they'll be separated from their panel or carrier, and design the outline to suit.

**Why:** Routers and V-score cutters are for rigid boards.
Flexible boards are cut with scissors or a knife at low volume, and with a laser at higher volume; each needs a suitable outline and access.

**Applies to:** Varies by manufacturer.
Check with yours.
